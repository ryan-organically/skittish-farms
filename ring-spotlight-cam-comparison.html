Key Differences Explained

3D Motion Detection (Pro Only)

This is the big one. The Pro uses actual radar—not just a heat sensor—to track motion. In practice, this means:

  • Bird's Eye View shows you exactly where motion traveled across your property, like you're watching from a drone
  • The camera knows if someone is approaching or leaving—direction matters
  • Fewer "your tree blew in the wind again" alerts that make you ignore the important ones
  • Better at telling a person from a dog from a car from a shadow

HDR Video (Pro Only)

Ever notice how outdoor photos either blow out the sky or make everything else too dark? HDR fixes that for video. You'll appreciate this when:

  • The sun is setting behind someone approaching your door (backlit faces become visible)
  • Your camera points at a mix of sunny lawn and shaded porch
  • You need to identify someone standing in a shadow while the background is bright

Dual-Band Wi-Fi (Pro Only)

If your home has a lot of smart devices, this matters more than you'd think. The Plus only uses 2.4GHz—the crowded band that everything from baby monitors to microwaves can interfere with. The Pro adds 5GHz support:

  • Faster, smoother live streaming when you're checking in
  • Better performance in homes with 20+ Wi-Fi devices
  • Falls back to 2.4GHz when you need the extra range

The Bottom Line

Get the Plus if you're covering a typical suburban side yard, back porch, or garage area. You'll get excellent security and save $60. Most people fall into this camp.

Get the Pro if you're frustrated by false alerts, have a large or unusually-shaped property, or just want the peace of mind that comes with having the best motion detection Ring offers.

Battery vs Solar Considerations

Here's something Ring doesn't advertise loudly: the Battery and Solar versions use the exact same camera. The "Solar" model just includes the charging panel in the box. So if you buy battery now, you can always add solar later for about $50.

Reality check on solar: You need 3-4 hours of direct sunlight daily to stay charged. Not "bright daylight"—actual sun hitting the panel. That shady north-facing side of your house? Solar's probably not your answer there. And winter in Seattle? You might still be pulling that battery inside occasionally.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I add a solar panel to any Ring Spotlight Cam?

Yep—and this is the move if you're not sure about solar. Buy the battery version now ($169 for Plus, $229 for Pro), and if you get tired of recharging, add the $49.99 solar panel later. Same result as buying the solar bundle, but you've tested whether your location actually gets enough sun first.

How bright are the spotlights?

400 lumens won't light up your backyard like a stadium, but that's kind of the point. It's bright enough to illuminate a pathway, startle someone who wasn't expecting it, and let the camera capture clear footage. Think "suddenly very visible" rather than "interrogation room." You can dim them or turn them off entirely in the app if the neighbors complain.

Does the siren actually deter intruders?

At 110dB, it's as loud as a car horn from about 3 feet away. Yes, it gets attention. Combined with spotlights snapping on, most people's instinct is to leave immediately. You can trigger it manually from your phone too—very satisfying when you catch a porch pirate in the act.

Is the Pro worth $60 more than the Plus?

For about 70% of people? Honestly, no. The Plus does the job well. The Pro makes sense if (a) you're constantly getting false alerts and it's making you ignore real ones, (b) you have a big, complicated property where tracking motion paths actually helps, or (c) you just want the best and $60 isn't a big deal. No shame in that last one.
