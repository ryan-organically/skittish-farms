How to Choose the Right Ring Camera

With 14 cameras to choose from, analysis paralysis is real. Answer these five questions and you'll narrow it down to 1-2 options fast.

Question 1: Indoor or Outdoor?

This is your first filter. Indoor cameras aren't weatherproof and shouldn't go outside. Outdoor cameras can technically work indoors, but you're paying for weatherproofing you don't need.

  • Indoor only: Indoor Cam, Pan-Tilt Cam
  • Outdoor only: Spotlight Cams, Floodlight Cams
  • Both: Stick Up Cam (the only true hybrid)

Question 2: Do You Have Existing Wiring?

This determines whether you can use hardwired cameras. No wiring? Battery models are your only option unless you want to hire an electrician.

  • Existing doorbell wiring: Any doorbell works, but Video Doorbell Pro 2 requires it
  • Existing outdoor electrical box: Floodlight Cams are an option
  • No wiring: Battery-powered cameras only (Video Doorbell 4, Stick Up Cam Battery, Spotlight Cam Battery)

Question 3: How Much Deterrence Do You Want?

Some cameras hide. Others announce themselves. How visible do you want your security to be?

  • Subtle monitoring: Indoor Cam, Stick Up Cam
  • Visible but compact: Video Doorbells
  • Active deterrence: Spotlight Cams (lights + siren)
  • Maximum deterrence: Floodlight Cams (unmissable)

Question 4: What's Your Budget?

Ring cameras range from $60 to $300. Here's what each price tier gets you:

  • Under $100: Basic 1080p video, essential features (Indoor Cam, Video Doorbell 2nd Gen, Stick Up Cam)
  • $100-$200: Better features like Pre-Roll, head-to-toe view, or spotlights (Video Doorbell 4, Spotlight Cam Plus)
  • $200+: Premium features like 3D Motion Detection and Bird's Eye View (Pro models)

Question 5: Do You Need Pre-Roll or Bird's Eye View?

These two features separate good Ring cameras from great ones.

Pre-Roll: Captures 4 seconds of video BEFORE the motion was detected. Instead of seeing someone walk away, you see them approach. Available on Video Doorbell 4 and higher.

Bird's Eye View: Uses radar to track movement across your property and displays the path on an overhead map. Shows exactly where someone walked. Available on Pro models only (Doorbell Pro 2, Spotlight Cam Pro, Floodlight Cam Pro).

Frequently Asked Questions

Do Ring cameras work without WiFi?

No. All Ring cameras require a WiFi connection to function. Without WiFi, you cannot view live video, receive notifications, or record footage. Some models can store limited video locally during brief outages, but extended offline use isn't possible.

Can I use Ring cameras without a subscription?

Technically yes, but it's severely limited. Without Ring Protect, you can only view live video and receive basic motion alerts. You cannot save, review, or share recordings. For most people, a Ring camera without a subscription defeats the purpose of having one.

How long do Ring camera batteries last?

Battery life varies based on usage, temperature, and settings. Typical ranges:

  • Light use (5-10 events/day): 4-6 months
  • Moderate use (15-25 events/day): 2-4 months
  • Heavy use (30+ events/day): 1-2 months

Cold weather (below 32F) significantly reduces battery life. Consider solar panels or plug-in options for high-traffic areas.

Are Ring cameras secure from hackers?

Ring has implemented strong security measures including end-to-end encryption, two-factor authentication, and regular security audits. However, no connected device is 100% hack-proof. Enable two-factor authentication, use a strong unique password, and keep firmware updated to minimize risk.

Can Ring cameras see at night?

Yes. All Ring cameras include infrared night vision that works in complete darkness. Some models also offer Color Night Vision, which produces color footage in low-light conditions (requires some ambient light source).

Do Ring cameras record continuously?

No. Ring cameras use motion-activated recording to conserve battery life and storage. They only record when motion is detected, though you can manually start Live View at any time. If you need 24/7 recording, consider wired cameras with a Ring Protect subscription and check the Snapshot Capture feature.
