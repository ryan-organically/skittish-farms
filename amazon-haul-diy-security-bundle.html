The 4 Layers of Budget Security

Real security isn't about one expensive gadget—it's about multiple layers that work together. Each layer makes your home a harder target. Here's what we're building:

  1. Surveillance - Eyes on your property 24/7
  2. Detection - Know immediately when something moves
  3. Deterrence - Make intruders think twice
  4. Physical barriers - Slow them down if they try anyway

Security Psychology: Most burglars are opportunistic. They're looking for easy targets. Even basic visible security measures (cameras, lights, alarm signs) cause most to move on to an easier house. You don't need Fort Knox—you need to be harder than your neighbors.

Setup Guide

Here's how to install your $50 security system in under an hour.

Install the Door/Window Alarms

Start with entry points. Peel the adhesive backing and stick the magnetic sensor on the frame, the alarm unit on the door. Test each one by opening the door—you should hear the alert immediately. Set to "alarm" mode when leaving, "chime" mode when home.

Position Your Indoor Camera

Place the WiFi camera where it can see your main entrance. Ideally at head height or higher, angled down. Download the app (usually CloudEdge or similar), connect to your 2.4GHz WiFi, and test live view. Insert a microSD card for local recording.

Set Up Motion Sensors

Place PIR motion sensors in hallways or rooms intruders would pass through after entering. Avoid pointing at windows (sunlight causes false alarms) or heat sources. Test walk patterns to ensure coverage of likely paths.

Install Outdoor Camera

Mount the wireless outdoor camera covering your driveway or back entrance. Charge the battery fully before mounting. Connect to your WiFi and test the spotlight trigger and motion detection zones through the app.

Deploy Solar Lights

Place solar lights along dark pathways and near entry points. Ensure the solar panels face south (in the Northern Hemisphere) for maximum charging. Let them charge for 24-48 hours before expecting full brightness.

Install Door Chain

Mount the door chain at comfortable height (around chest level). Use all provided screws and drill pilot holes if your door frame is hardwood. Test that the chain catches properly when the door opens partially.
